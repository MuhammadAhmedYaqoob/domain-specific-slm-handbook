# 2. ULTIMATE PROJECT OBJECTIVE

The final objective is to build a scalable, repeatable platform for developing multiple domain-specific Small Language Models (SLMs).

Example domains:

- Car and automotive mechanics.
- Motorcycle and motorbike technicians.
- Heavy-duty truck and commercial vehicle mechanics.
- Pharmacy and pharmaceutical knowledge.
- Beekeeping and apiary management.
- Zookeeping, animal care, and animal husbandry.
- Agricultural equipment maintenance.
- Industrial machinery and manufacturing.
- Electronics and electrical repair.
- Other specialized technical and professional fields.

Each domain may ultimately have its own model, adapter, evaluation suite, deployment artifact, and model version.

Models must be capable of understanding domain terminology, answering specialist questions, explaining technical concepts, performing appropriate reasoning, and supporting diagnostic or knowledge-oriented workflows.

**The primary goal is to build genuinely specialized models whose domain capabilities are acquired through training, not through runtime document retrieval.**

At the end of this book, the reader should be capable of:

1. Selecting an appropriate pretrained SLM.

3. Cleaning, structuring, deduplicating, labeling, and validating that data.

5. Fine-tuning models through multiple approaches.
6. Experimenting with quantization, pruning, distillation, and other optimization methods.
7. Combining compatible optimization techniques.
8. Evaluating model quality and computational efficiency.
9. Building specialized models for multiple domains.
10. Running optimized models entirely offline on compatible mobile devices.
11. Deploying the same or different optimized models on AWS.
12. Comparing cloud, edge, and optional hybrid inference architectures.
13. Building reproducible MLOps workflows for future specialized models.

# 4. NO RUNTIME RAG: NON-NEGOTIABLE REQUIREMENT

The primary solution must not use Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG).

Do not implement the core product using:

- Vector databases.
- Runtime document retrieval.
- Embedding search over external knowledge stores.
- Retrieval pipelines.
- Dynamic document chunk selection.
- Retrieval-based prompt augmentation.

Domain knowledge must primarily be learned through training and represented in the model parameters or appropriately trained adapters.

Permitted:

- Collecting external information to prepare training datasets.
- Using technical documents as offline training material.
- Using teacher models to synthesize examples.
- Using experts to validate training examples.
- Using external ground-truth material during evaluation.
- Using datasets and search tools during the research and development stages.
- Using deterministic tools for permitted non-retrieval operations when explicitly required.

Not permitted in the primary inference architecture:

- Fetching domain documents to generate each answer.
- Calling a remote teacher model to answer questions on behalf of the edge SLM.
- Quietly replacing domain learning with a retrieval system.

Explain the practical limitations of relying on learned model parameters, including imperfect factual recall, stale knowledge, domain conflicts, hallucinations, and safety concerns.

Be explicit that model training does not guarantee exact storage of every fact.

For sensitive areas such as pharmaceutical knowledge and safety-critical equipment repair, include appropriate verification requirements and limits on autonomous advice.
